Should you list every cheap Magic card or sift your bulk first

Market analysis TCG business TCGplayer

Sam at Leumas Card Craze asks what he would do if he woke up tomorrow with no inventory, no scanner, no storefront, just his knowledge and 1,000 dollars. He would not start by buying collections or sealed product. He would buy a Ricoh scanner and list everything, even the cards worth a few cents. Ten days in, he has listed 11,000 cards.

He learned that the hard way. He bought a 3,000 dollar collection and watched the market drop before he could move it, and he followed YouTubers who told him to mark everything up 20 percent. Then he sold almost nothing.

His 10 day experiment produced the interesting numbers. Across eBay, TCGplayer, ManaPool and CardTrader he listed 17,783 cards and sold 3,017 for 1,742 dollars, clearing 1,261 dollars in profit, 42 cents a card. The cheapest bucket, cards worth 1 to 20 cents, sold for 203 percent of market value. Buyers paid the shipping, and shipping is where the profit lives. He puts it plainly, he is making more on shipping than on the actual cards.

Look closer, though. Those sub 20 cent cards made 18 cents profit each, and only because they sold in bulk quantities. On TCGplayer, 358 of the 438 sold in that bucket came in just four orders that carried shipping. Single cheap cards do not pay, so he has raised his TCGplayer floor to 35 cents to slow sales down.

There is a catch. His examples are Pokemon, not Magic. The mechanics still transfer, but Magic bulk has its own supply problem. Roka sifters have flooded TCGplayer with listings, and bulk staple prices have been sliding for weeks. Listing everything pays when few sellers do it at scale. When everyone does it, the cards keep getting cheaper.

For anyone selling Magic, the takeaway is narrow. List the cheap cards if you can move them in multi card orders where the buyer covers shipping. Do not list them one at a time, and do not pay to package a 2 cent card for a 25 cent sale. Decide the model first. Scanner first, collections later.

Sources: Leumas Card Craze on YouTube and Why are bulk card prices crashing on TCGplayer in 2026.
