Is the Extra Life 2026 Secret Lair worth buying

Market analysis Secret Lair Extra Life

MTG Metrics posted a review of the new Extra Life Secret Lair today and called it another strong one. The drop is called Rise of the Polygons, it went on sale this morning, and the claim is easy to check because the card list is short and the prices are public.

Four cards and a token. Balefire Dragon, Big Score, Young Pyromancer, and Sword of Truth and Justice, plus an Elemental token, all in low-poly late 90s video game art by Brendan Sullivan. The non-foil is 39.99 dollars, the foil is 49.99 dollars, and it is print to demand from November 16 to November 30, so there is no queue to fight and no sellout to chase.

The value checks out. The cheapest copies of the four cards add up to roughly 44 to 50 dollars on the singles market, depending on whose math you trust. Sword of Truth and Justice does most of the work at around 28 to 31 dollars, Balefire Dragon adds about 15 to 18 dollars, and Big Score and Young Pyromancer are under a dollar combined. So the non-foil roughly pays for itself before you count the art or the charity. Half of the purchase price, less transaction fees, goes to Extra Life for Seattle Children's Hospital, which means about 20 dollars of a non-foil order is a donation.

The catch is the same one every charity drop has. Do not buy it as a flip. Print to demand means anyone who wants one can get one for two weeks, so there is no scarcity premium waiting on the other side. Buy the non-foil if you want the Sword and the Dragon for your decks, buy the foil if you love the art, and treat the rest as a donation with nice cards attached. That is the whole pitch, and for once the math backs it up.

Sources: Wizards of the Coast on Extra Life 2026, Wargamer on the card values, and TheGamer on the bundle.
