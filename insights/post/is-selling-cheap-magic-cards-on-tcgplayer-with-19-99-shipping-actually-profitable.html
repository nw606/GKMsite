Is selling cheap Magic cards on TCGplayer with 19.99 shipping actually profitable

Selling cards TCGplayer Shipping

Leumas Card Craze kicked off a 45-day experiment this week that every small card seller should be watching. He is listing cards at TCGplayer Low, charging 19.99 dollars shipping per order, and trying to package 1,000 cards a day. Will buyers pay 19.99 shipping for cheap cards, and does the workflow pay after the hours?

The shipping charge is doing most of the work. Take a buyer who grabs 40 cards at an average Low of 50 cents. That is 20 dollars of cards plus 19.99 shipping, just under 40 dollars total. TCGplayer takes a 10.75 percent commission on the cards and the shipping, plus a 2.5 percent transaction fee and 30 cents per order. That is about 5.60 dollars in fees. Postage on a 40-card tracked package runs maybe 5 to 7 dollars. The seller keeps roughly 27 dollars on an order where the cards were worth 20 dollars at Low. The shipping charge, not the cards, is the margin.

That only works if buyers actually place those orders. TCGplayer sorts listings by price plus shipping, so a 50 cent card with 19.99 shipping looks terrible to anyone buying one or two cards. The model only makes sense for big basket orders, which are exactly the buyers a bulk seller wants. Leumas works mostly in Pokemon, but the economics are identical for Magic. The open question is how many basket buyers exist at 19.99 shipping when other sellers charge a dollar or two.

There is a reason TCGplayer itself prices cheap cards the way it does. Its Direct program charges 50 percent of a card's value on anything under 2.49 dollars, because handling a cheap card costs nearly as much as handling an expensive one. Leumas is testing whether a marketplace seller can solve the same problem with a shipping charge instead. If 45 days of data says yes, sellers sitting on boxes of bulk will rethink how they list. If buyers balk, it is another reminder that cheap cards only sell when the total cost to the buyer stays low.
