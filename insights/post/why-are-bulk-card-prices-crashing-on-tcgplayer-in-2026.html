Why are bulk card prices crashing on TCGplayer in 2026

Market analysis TCGplayer TCG business

Mason at Card N All Gaming says the machines did it. TCGplayer's Roca sifters landed about two months ago, and in his new video he argues they did exactly what he feared when he first tested one. Instead of just making sorting faster for established shops, they dropped the barrier to entry for buying bulk collections and listing them. Anyone with a machine can now run thousands of cards through in an afternoon and put them straight on TCGplayer.

The numbers are hard to argue with. He compared current TCGplayer listings against snapshots from about two months ago. Dunsparce from Journey Together went from 177 listings at a 2 dollar market price to 1,460 listings at 1.17 dollars. Thwackey fell harder, from 389 listings at 1.11 dollars to 1,005 listings at 30 cents. Metang dropped from 3.78 dollars on 174 listings to 1.60 on 795. The worst case might be Night Stretcher, a staple in nearly every deck, now sitting at 2,153 listings and a 25 cent market price, with 16,475 near mint copies listed against about 57 sales a day. That works out to roughly 288 days of supply already on the shelf, and more keeps arriving.

Not every card drowned. Mist Energy actually went the other way, from 634 listings down to 450 with the price up, and Mason admits he has no explanation for it. That is worth remembering before treating the flood as a law of nature. Demand still moves individual cards.

Mason says Magic is seeing the same flooding. For anyone selling, a card's market price alone is not enough anymore. Check the days of supply before you list. If a card has months of copies already sitting there, the price will keep sliding while yours waits in line. For buyers, it is the opposite story. Playable bulk has rarely been cheaper. The machines are great news if you are building decks and bad news if you are trying to flip a pile of bulk for profit without one.

Sources: Card N All Gaming on YouTube and TCGplayer.
