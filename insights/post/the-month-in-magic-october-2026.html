The Month in Magic: October 2026

Market analysis Sealed product Reprints Price moves

October was the month the sealed market stopped looking simple. Demand stayed real, yet almost every easy story ran into a supply announcement, a fee, or a second wave of product. Four threads explain most of the month: sealed strength, reprints as a regular tool, cheap singles that only worked in baskets, and a ban announcement that changed nothing yet still moved a price.

Sealed stayed strong, with a split down the middle

Reality Fracture told the October story. On October 11, its play booster box sat at 130 dollars against a 189 dollar bundle, while its collector display sat at 433 dollars against a 324 dollar MSRP. Play boosters were the deal. Collector product carried a premium that was already compressing as more supply landed.

Then came the shipping story. An October 21 analysis confirmed the Reality Fracture delay, with product delayed in transit, and predicted a second wave, including a possible play booster reprint. By October 22, the bundle held at about 140 dollars on TCGplayer, above its 89.99 dollar original price but below the 150 dollar year-end target. Supply arrives in waves, and each wave reprices the last one.

The month also had a genuine sellout. Festival in a Box Atlanta 2026 went on sale on October 19 and sold out in about 20 minutes. The Mystery Booster Commander Edition box inside it, the first of its kind and not sold separately at retail, was listed at 375 to 400 dollars on eBay after the sale.

Reprints stopped being a surprise

The biggest structural news was the Warhammer 40,000 Commander reprint. Wizards confirmed to IGN on October 8 that the four 2022 decks, originally 59.99 dollars MSRP, would get a small reprint run, likely arriving in early 2027. It was the first out-of-print Universes Beyond Commander product ever reprinted. Resale prices dipped on rumors alone. By October 27, the reprint was reported as US-only, with Collector's Editions excluded, and two decks had already crept back up about 10 dollars.

Reality Fracture gave the other reprint story. Its delayed standalone bundle, originally 89.99 dollars, was routed through the Secret Lair Chaos Vault instead, going live on October 26 at 159.98 dollars for nonfoil, 179.98 dollars for foil, and 234.99 dollars for everything. A delayed product did not disappear. It reappeared at a higher price, through a different channel.

Movers: spikes that held, and deflation that did not stop

October split cleanly between cards that spiked and stayed spiked, and cards that deflated exactly on schedule.

On the spike side, Bronzebeak Foragers moved from below 1 dollar to over 3 dollars in the week of October 24 to 30, on cEDH adoption. Dark Matter Manipulator moved from 0.75 dollars on October 7 to about 7.50 dollars on October 23, on Modern Rakdos Delirium demand, with more than 2,150 TCGplayer sales clearing supply down to 42 near-mint listings. The Theorist, Jace Beleren climbed from 50.78 dollars on October 12 to 75.05 dollars on October 16, across Commander, cEDH, Standard, and Modern. These were demand waves with a second leg, not one-day blips.

On the deflation side, Sphinx's Approach did exactly what new commons do. A day-one price of 2.06 dollars fell to 0.58 dollars by early November, below the 1 dollar line. Craterclaw Colossus foil, which had a prerelease market of 99 dollars against a 34.99 dollar buylist, settled below 50 dollars once supply opened. Release deflation is the default, and play demand is what beats it.

Two fade calls went the wrong way, and both are instructive. Words of Worship was expected to fall below 15 dollars by October 31. It closed at 21.85 to 23.47 dollars. Baleful Strix was expected to fall below 5 dollars. It closed at about 13 dollars. The reason ties directly to the month's ban news.

The ban announcement that changed prices by changing nothing

The October 12 Banned and Restricted update made no Pauper changes. Baleful Strix had sat at 6.05 dollars on September 5, with a ban overhang. Once the update left it legal, with the panel saying nothing was immediately a problem and a check back in December, Pauper demand stayed intact and the price climbed to about 13 dollars.

Selling lessons: baskets, fees, and the workflow bottleneck

October was a good month for unglamorous lessons. The clearest came from a Pokemon-focused list-everything experiment by Leumas Card Craze, tracked on October 12. Over ten days, 21 orders and 84 cards produced 80.42 dollars in revenue and 47.29 dollars in profit, about 0.56 dollars per card and 2.25 dollars per order, at a 58.8 percent margin. The mechanics were bulk cost of goods around 2 cents per card, supplies bought in bulk, about 3.3 minutes of packaging per order, and cart rules pushing buyers from one card toward five to eight cards.

The catch matters more. Nothing in that data shows a literal 1 dollar single shipped solo turning a profit, and the eBay fee floor on a 1 dollar sale is about 43 cents before postage, supplies, or labor. Sub-dollar singles monetize inside multi-card baskets, not as solo products. The lever is cart size and order minimums. One caveat: only about 2,500 of the roughly 63,000 bulk cards acquired in two weeks were Magic, so sell-through rates from a Pokemon experiment do not transfer directly.

Fees stayed front and center. A standard TCGplayer marketplace order costs 10.75 percent commission plus a 2.5 percent transaction fee and 30 cents per order, roughly 13.5 percent all in. Then, on October 27, TCGplayer announced a tracked envelope option, rolling out to sellers in early November, aimed at lower-value singles. Coverage was skeptical. The honest position is to watch the actual cost and format once it launches, not to assume it fixes the cheap-card math.

The scorecard, and what to watch in November

Eight predictions were scored around the end of October, with five right and three wrong, for a 62 percent hit rate. The wins were mostly deflation and floor calls: Maelstrom Colossus under 1 dollar, Giant's Boulder at 0.28 dollars, Seeker of Skybreak below 2.50 dollars by a single cent, Craterclaw Colossus foil below 50 dollars, and Sphinx's Approach below 1 dollar. The misses were the fade calls that did not fade, Words of Worship and Baleful Strix, plus the Baleful Strix legality call itself.

November has plenty to resolve. Star Trek releases in December, with open questions on whether its collector display holds above its 694.74 dollar preorder level and whether any of its four Commander decks, at 74.99 dollars MSRP each, trades above list. The Reality Fracture bundle question runs to December 31, against its 150 dollar target. The tracked envelope rollout lands in the first week of November, and the December Banned and Restricted check back will test whether Baleful Strix and Pauper stay calm.

October did not end the bull market, and it did not confirm it either. It showed a market where demand is real, supply is no longer a one-time event, and the sellers who win are the ones who price in the next wave before it arrives.
