Why are Star Trek Magic booster boxes selling below what stores pay

Market analysis Sealed product Star Trek

Louie at Kitchen Table TCG runs a game store in West Virginia, and his latest video asks whether the Amazon dumps are back. His answer is worth hearing, because it is not the one the title suggests.

Star Trek is the latest example. Wizards put Play Booster boxes on Amazon below distributor cost before the set even released on November 13, while stores had locked in distributor prices months earlier for an allocation. For players that is a win right now. For a store that paid about 121 dollars wholesale and watches the same box sell for 104 dollars, it is a loss on every box.

Louie has seen this before. He walks through the Brothers War, Dominaria United and Dominaria Remastered dumps, the shops that closed, and his store pivoting hard into singles to survive. He argues there should be a floor on boxes sold online. But he does not think Star Trek means the dumps are back. His read is simpler: Star Trek overstated its demand, the third Universes Beyond set since June, and supply stayed high while buyers stayed away.

The numbers back him up, and they suggest the panic may have peaked. A Play Booster box held 152 to 157 dollars on TCGplayer through early October, then fell to 130 dollars in preview season, 115 dollars on November 2 and 107 dollars the day before release. It bottomed near 103 dollars after release, then climbed to about 111 dollars by November 24. Amazon followed it down to 104.99 dollars, about 30 cents above TCGplayer market. A store selling at 107 dollars clears roughly 84 to 86 dollars after the 12.75 percent fee and shipping, about 35 dollars under wholesale.

The takeaway depends on which side you are on. Buying to open or draft, a box at 111 dollars holds roughly 169 to 175 dollars in cards, real value if you will actually sell or play the singles. Buying to flip sealed, wait. The box has already bounced 8 percent off its low, and a demand miss is not the same as a dump cycle.

Sources: Kitchen Table TCG on YouTube, The Cardboard Chronicles, Spellbook Finance and NerdSnack.
