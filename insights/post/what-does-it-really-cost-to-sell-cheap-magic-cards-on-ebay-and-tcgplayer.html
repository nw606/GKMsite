What does it really cost to sell cheap Magic cards on eBay and TCGplayer

Selling cards TCGplayer Shipping

Leumas Card Craze is three days into listing 1,000 trading cards a day, and day three is the one for Magic sellers. It tallies the hidden costs of selling cheap cards: not just the marketplace fee, but scanning, storage, inventory, packaging, and shipping. Then the question is whether the time and money are actually worth it.

Day one asked whether 19.99 dollar shipping could carry the whole model. The fees alone tell most of the story. On eBay the trading card fee is 13.25 percent of everything the buyer pays, including shipping and tax, not just the card price. Add a 30 or 40 cent per-order fee and a one dollar card sold on its own is underwater before it ships. TCGplayer takes a similar cut, and the cheapest tracked shipping, the eBay Standard Envelope, runs under a dollar twenty but only helps when cards share one envelope.

Then there is the time nobody bills for. Scanning a thousand cards a day, sleeving them, storing them so you can find one three weeks later, then pulling and packing orders. That is what the experiment is really testing. Leumas ends each video with his actual sales across eBay, TCGplayer, ManaPool, and Card Trader, so the numbers will settle it.

The interesting part is the contrast he draws. Listing everything bets buyers will find the exact cheap card they need and order several at once. Sifting bets the opposite, that most cheap cards cost more to handle than they return, and only the ones with demand earn a listing. The difference is whether your costs are mostly fees, which scale with the sale, or mostly time, which does not.

Before you list sub-dollar cards, do the math on one order, not one card. Add the fee on the full total, the per-order charge, the envelope and sleeve, and a minute of handling at what your hour is worth. If it still pays, list it. If it only pays with five cards in one envelope, that is the real minimum order size, and anything below it is bulk.

Sources: Leumas Card Craze on YouTube and our look at day one of the experiment.
