Is Hasbro printing too many Magic Star Trek boxes for sellers to make money

Star Trek Play booster boxes Sealed product Buying sealed

AnonTCG took Hasbro CEO Chris Cocks at his word this week and found it wanting. On the October 20 earnings call Cocks said Wizards manages Magic for the fans, not for quarterly results. Anon says the Star Trek print run tells a different story, a Marvel-sized run pushed out the door to book the revenue before year end, with play boxes already near 115 dollars and sellers about 30 dollars underwater on every box.

The quote checks out, and so does the context around it. Magic just posted its first 600 million dollar quarter, 616 million in Q3 and up 34 percent on the year, and Cocks told analysts to expect Q4 down, partly because sales meant for Q4 landed in Q3. The earnings call notes back him up on both points. The Star Trek numbers back Anon up too. Our sealed watch list has the play box market at 115.75 dollars with deals at 114.98, and a distributor charging 121.50 a box, which is already above the market price before fees or shipping. The collector box tells the same story from the other end, down from a 694.74 preorder level to 501.98. Even the box value calculators cannot agree, with one putting the play box near 198 dollars and another near 109.

Both things can be true at once. Hasbro can mean the fans line and still print to the size of the demand it expects, and when the print run overshoots that demand the sellers eat it, not the publisher. Frank's Hobbies made the other case the same day, that Universes Beyond is far from done, and he has a point, since Lord of the Rings and Final Fantasy kept selling long after their own wobbles. For buyers the read is simple. A 115 dollar play box is a fine box to draft with friends and a bad box to flip. For sellers, a print run this big this close to the holidays is a warning, not a buying opportunity, and the next print decision will matter more than the last earnings quote.
