How far has the Magic the Gathering market fallen and when should you buy

Market analysis Market downturn Sealed product

Final Trade asked the question a lot of sellers are asking right now in a video this week: how far has the Magic market actually fallen. He works through it the slow way, lining up the numbers instead of just reading the mood. The mood is grim, but the numbers are what matter, and they tell a more useful story than the gloom does.

Start with the set everyone is watching. Star Trek hits shelves today, and the box prices have been sliding for a week before release. The play booster display was 111.97 dollars on November 9 and 107.28 on November 12. Today it sits at 104.41 dollars on TCGplayer market, with a low of 102. The collector display fell from 454.54 on November 11 to 426.18 on November 12 and 411.81 today. That is a set dropping through its own predicted floor on release day, not bouncing off it.

It is not just Star Trek. Across the sealed products we track, 13 of 15 fell over the last month, and the only two that rose were Final Fantasy and Avatar play boxes. The singles side explains a lot of it. Star Trek singles were down 95 to 99 percent before release as prerelease supply hit the market, so the box price had nowhere to go but down. When the cards inside a box are worth less every day, the box follows.

The takeaway is not that the market is dead, it is that falling prices punish different people in different ways. If you buy sealed to hold, a falling market is a reason to wait for a price that matches the contents of the box, not to catch every dip on the way down. If you sell, the lesson runs the other way. Boxes that slid all through prerelease rarely bounce on release day, so the money was in selling into the prerelease hype, not in holding through the launch.
