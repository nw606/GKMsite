Is the Magic Star Trek collector booster box worth buying at 482 dollars

Star Trek Collector booster boxes Sealed product Buying sealed Universes Beyond

eugigamez ran the numbers on the Star Trek collector box this week, and his answer is blunt. At the 482 dollar presale price, no. Not even if you are a hardcore Star Trek fan. He would only reconsider if boxes fall to 320 to 340 dollars, and only then for a fan willing to sit on them for two to five years. His guess is that they get under 400 dollars before long.

The math inside the box is the problem. The most expensive regular card in the set is the USS Enterprise D Galaxy Class extended art at about 23 dollars. The best full art shock land is around 18 dollars. Even a lucky box with a headliner or a surge foil does not come close to 482 dollars. A box holds twelve packs at a 37.99 dollar sticker, so 456 dollars is the real anchor price, and the presale is still above it.

When I checked the market, the slide he describes is already happening. The TCGplayer market price for the box sat around 526 dollars over the last two weeks, down about 10 percent from the two weeks before that, with nearly 1,400 boxes sold. The lowest listing was 470 dollars on November 4, and the most recent sale was about 474 dollars. The price had spiked to 576 dollars right after Patrick Stewart died, which says a lot of the presale heat was grief and headline chasing rather than steady demand for the cards themselves.

That leaves a simple read. If you want to open a box, wait until after the November 13 release and let the price keep falling. If you hold presale stock, the market is telling you the premium is already gone, so moving it beats waiting for a bounce. And if you were buying sealed to hold, eugigamez is right to point at the Hobbit set, which was far more hyped and still fell from around 900 dollars to about 565. Star Trek is not the Hobbit, and the box price knows it.

Source: eugigamez, Is It Worth It To Buy The Star Trek Collector Box?
