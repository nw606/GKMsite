Is the Magic the Gathering bull market over after three years of rising sealed prices

Sealed boxes Magic prices Market downturn Alpha Investments

Alpha Investments says the collectibles bull market that started in January 2024 is over. He puts it at 35 months, almost three full years, and he expects the next 30 to 90 days to be volatile and messy. That sounds like a crash call, but he pulls it back himself. He says this is not a crash to zero. He calls it a return to normal, and he admits the title is dramatic on purpose.

His argument is mostly about supply, not about players quitting. He says distributors and stores suddenly started offering big piles of recent product that they would not have offered a couple years ago. The examples he gives are all sealed boxes, and they are ugly for anyone who bought near release. Reality Fracture collector boxes are down to about 375 dollars from about 590 dollars at release. The Hobbit collector boxes fell from a 900 dollar peak to about 600 dollars in 45 days. Star Trek play boxes sit around 125 dollars against a wholesale cost around 120 dollars, before fees and shipping, which leaves a seller underwater once a marketplace takes its cut.

When I looked closer, the picture splits in a useful way. Alpha says older sets like Duskmourn and Bloomburrow did not really fall, and he puts Bloomburrow collector boxes at an all time high. None of that is a single card price, and none of it is checked against sales data in the video itself. It is one big seller talking about what distributors are offering him right now, which is a real signal but not a full market read.

So the takeaway is narrower than the headline. Do not dump older scarce boxes because a new release fell apart. Do not buy a recent play box just because it looks cheap next to wholesale, either. Run the all in math first, with shipping and fees included, and ask if you can sit on it for years if it keeps sliding. If the answer is no, the cheap box is not a deal yet.
