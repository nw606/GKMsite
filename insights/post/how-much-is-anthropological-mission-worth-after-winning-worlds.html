How much is Anthropological Mission worth after winning the Magic World Championship

Market analysis Star Trek

A week ago, Anthropological Mission was a three cent uncommon from a brand new Star Trek set. Then it won the Magic World Championship.

Stefan Schütz played four copies in his Izzet Mission deck at MagicCon Atlanta, went 10-0 in Standard, and swept the final 3-0. The card went from several cents to around five dollars overnight, according to Draftsim, and TCGplayer now lists it at $5.88, the third most expensive bulk card in Standard. It is also the best selling card of the week on TCGplayer, ahead of every mythic in the set.

The deck is simple to explain and hard to stop. Anthropological Mission draws a card every time you cast a noncreature spell, once you have done it three times and the discard clause falls off. Schütz built around it with one mana spells like Opt and Flashback, plus Ral, Crackling Wit as the finisher. Only two players in a 128 person field registered the deck, and it still won the whole event.

So is five dollars the real price, or the Worlds weekend price? There is a decent case for it holding. The card is a four-of in a proven Standard deck, and it is also the kind of draw engine Commander players will want in spellslinger decks. TCGplayer's own seller blog notes the card's snowball effect as the reason it is in demand.

The case against it is supply. Star Trek is print to demand, and every pack opened from here on adds more copies to the market. Cards that spike on a single tournament result have a habit of cooling off once everyone has had a chance to buy in, and the deck will draw sideboard hate now that it is public.

My read is that the card settles above where it started but below the spike. If you pulled one, this is a good week to list it. If you want to play the deck, wait a couple of weeks and see where the price lands once the Worlds buzz fades.

Sources: Wizards on the World Championship 32 Standard results, Draftsim on the weekly price spikes, TCGplayer on the most expensive bulk cards, and TCGplayer on the best-selling Star Trek cards.
