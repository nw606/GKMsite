Should you sell Magic cards at all-time highs in October 2026

Reality Fracture Commander staples Selling cards Card prices Planeswalkers

Joel from Joel are Magic put out a video this week framing ten cards as a sell list for October. Most of them are up sharply because new Reality Fracture commanders want them, and his advice is blunt. Sell spare copies while the price is there, then put the money toward cards you will play. That is bolder than the usual hold-and-hope instinct, and it is worth taking seriously.

The lead example shows why. Nicol Bolas, Dragon-God was around $5 a month ago in Joel's numbers and about $41 when he filmed, after topping near $45. Planeswalker decks built around Tam and Jace want a card that copies every loyalty ability on your side of the board. Outside trackers back up the shape of the move, with Bolas climbing through the teens and into the high $20s in early October.

The useful part is the gap between retail and buylist. Joel pairs retail lows with Card Conduit figures, and for several cards the buylist sits well below the headline price. Listing a card yourself can get closer to retail, but fees and shipping eat into that, and a price already slipping from its peak can fall faster than a listing sells. A buylist offer looks low until you compare it with what you would actually keep.

There is a warning in the video that should not get skipped. The Reality Fracture precons were only about three weeks old when Joel filmed, and he admits last month he called a spike before product was fully in people's hands. Some of these cards will stick because the decks stick. Others will fade once everyone who wanted a copy has one. Joel himself expects Bolas to fall back toward $20 by March, which is a useful reminder that an all-time high is a moment, not a floor.

So sell spares into strength, and do not chase the headline if you are buying. Wait for retail and buylist numbers to settle, check how many printings exist, and ask whether the driving deck will still be popular in a few months.
