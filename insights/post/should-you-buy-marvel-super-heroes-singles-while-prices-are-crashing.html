Should you buy Marvel Super Heroes singles while prices are crashing

Marvel Super Heroes Singles prices Commander cards Buying singles

Joel Are Magic says Marvel Super Heroes singles are falling faster than Spider-Man, Avatar, or Lorwyn Eclipsed did at the same age, and he treats this as a crash buying guide rather than a reason to panic. His headline numbers are hard to ignore. The Mind Stone went from 69 dollars in mid July to about 42 dollars. The Ten Rings fell from 27 dollars to 10 dollars. Mjolnir dropped from 30 dollars to 16 dollars. Base mythics are down 40 to 55 percent, and Joel says the set is still falling, with the floor about a dollar away on most of what you would actually buy.

When I looked closer, the breadth of the fall stood out more than any single card. Every base rare and mythic adds up to about 179 dollars, and 53 of those 85 cards are under a dollar. A current set snapshot puts the average rare at 69 cents and the full nonfoil set at about 226 dollars. This is not one chase card dragging a headline down. Most of the set is cheap now, and a lot of it is Commander playable.

Joel splits his advice in a useful way. For collector pieces, buy inside this window and before April, when Marvel Darkhold Destiny arrives on April 9. His receipt is Spider-Man, where the Soul Stone went from 62 dollars to 136 dollars in the three weeks after Super Heroes released. For base playables, buy them when the dollar does not matter to you. Do not try to time the last dollar if you actually want the card for a deck.

The takeaway is to separate the two jobs. If you are collecting fancy treatments, the window has a deadline. If you are filling out decks, a falling market is your friend, and waiting for a perfect bottom usually costs more in missed games than it saves. For a seller, do not chase the fall with fresh stock at old prices, and do not hold bulk rares hoping for a bounce that needs a new set first.
